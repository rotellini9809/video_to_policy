Goalkeeper RL – Full Training Pipeline (Working Document)

Scope: This document defines the entire RL pipeline for a goalkeeper agent using a latent action space ********z, hierarchical skills, and priors.Sim2Real is NOT addressed explicitly here, but architectural choices are made so it remains possible later.

This is a living document meant to be edited iteratively.

0. High-Level Overview

The pipeline is structured as a hierarchy:

Ball / World State
        ↓
High-level Policy  π_GK(o_t) → z_t
        ↓
Low-level Motor Controller  π_motor(s_body, z_t)
        ↓
Joint torques / targets

Learning is decomposed into four stages:

Low-level motor controller (fixed)

Goalkeeper skill experts (task-specific)

Goalkeeper skill priors (task-agnostic)

Final goalkeeper policy (full task)

Only Stage 4 is the final deployed policy.

1. Stage 1 — Low-Level Motor Controller (Fixed)

1.1 Purpose

The low-level controller is responsible for:

balance

locomotion

recovery from falls

basic whole-body coordination

It does not understand the task, the ball, or the goal.

1.2 Interface

π_motor(a_t | s_body, z_t)

s_body: proprioception (joint pos/vel, IMU, contacts)

z_t: latent motor intention (continuous)

a_t: joint targets / torques

This policy is:

trained once

frozen forever after training

1.3 Training Method (Abstract)

Any of the following are acceptable:

imitation learning from motion

RL on locomotion & recovery drills

hybrid methods

Only requirement:

random z samples must produce stable, smooth, physically valid motion.

2. Stage 2 — Goalkeeper Skill Experts

2.1 Purpose

Skill experts learn task-specific goalkeeper behaviors in simplified environments.

They answer the question:

How should a goalkeeper move in this very specific situation?

The experts are designed so that the goalkeeper:

stays near the goalpost area at all times

prioritizes blocking the ball when it approaches the goal

clears the ball away from the goal area after contact

2.2 Locked Expert Skill Set (No Dives)

For the first full pipeline iteration, the expert set is explicitly locked without dives.

The initial expert set is:

E1. Set & Square Expert

Maintain a ready goalkeeper stance

Face and align torso toward the incoming ball

Small posture adjustments without translation

E2. Step–Intercept Expert

Short lateral and forward steps

Position the body on the ball’s path

Movement constrained to the goalkeeper area

E3. Block / Deflect Expert

Upright blocking mechanics

Bracing, limb placement, torso orientation

Prevent the ball from crossing the goal line

E4. Clear-Away Expert (Punch or Kick)

After contact or when the ball is in the danger zone:

redirect the ball away from the goal

increase distance from the goal over time

The expert is not constrained to a specific modality:

punching, kicking, or body deflection are all valid

E5. Recovery Expert

Get up from fallen or unstable poses

Return to a stable ready stance

Re-center within the goalkeeper area

Diving behaviors are intentionally excluded at this stage.

2.3 Goalkeeper Area and Danger Zone (Conceptual)

Two spatial regions are assumed throughout all expert drills:

Goalkeeper Area:A bounded region in front of the goal where the goalkeeper is expected to remain.
Leaving this area is discouraged or penalized.

Danger Zone:A smaller region close to the goal mouth where the ball represents an immediate scoring threat.
Clear and block rewards are activated primarily when the ball enters this zone.

Precise geometric definitions (sizes, coordinates, thresholds) are deferred and will be specified later once field and robot dimensions are finalized.

2.4 Expert Drill Environments

Each expert is trained in its own dedicated drill environment.

Common properties:

single agent

short horizon (≈ 0.5–1.5 s)

simplified ball launcher

restricted variability

goalkeeper constrained to remain near the goal

2.5 Expert Observations (Privileged)

Experts may observe:

proprioception

ball relative position and velocity

privileged future information (e.g., intercept point, predicted trajectory)

optional timing signals

Privileged information is allowed only at the expert level.

2.6 Expert Outputs

Experts output latent intentions:

π_expert(z_t | o_expert)

Actions are produced exclusively through the frozen low-level motor controller.

2.7 Reward Design for Experts (Simple & Concrete)

Rewards are intentionally simple and outcome-focused.

Across experts:

positive reward for preventing the ball from crossing the goal line

negative reward for conceding a goal

penalty for leaving the goalkeeper area

penalty for falling or remaining prone

Expert-specific emphasis:

Set & Square: alignment to ball, posture stability

Step–Intercept: reducing distance to ball path inside goalkeeper area

Block / Deflect: stopping or redirecting ball away from goal

Clear-Away: increasing ball distance from goal after contact

Recovery: fast return to standing and stable posture

No complex shaping or dense geometric rewards are used at this stage.

2.8 About Diving Behaviors

Diving behaviors are deliberately postponed.

Rationale:

dives are high-commitment, irreversible actions

adding them too early destabilizes learning

early experts should emphasize discipline and positioning

The plan is to:

first train and evaluate the full pipeline without dives

assess save rate, stability, and clearing effectiveness

introduce dive experts later only if necessary, as additional experts

3. Stage 3 — Distillation into Skill Priors

3.1 Purpose of Priors

Skill priors are task-agnostic motion patterns.

They encode:

how to dive

how to step

how to recover

They do not encode:

when to act

strategy

goal awareness

3.2 Prior Observation Space (Restricted)

Each prior observes less than its expert:

Included:

proprioception

ball relative position & velocity

Removed:

goal position

intercept target

predicted trajectory

drill-specific signals

This restriction is intentional.

3.3 Distillation Dataset

Collect rollouts from each expert across:

different ball trajectories

different speeds and angles

different initial body poses

Dataset consists of:

(h_full, z_expert)

3.4 Distillation Objective

Distill in latent space:

L_prior = E[ KL( π_expert(z | h_full)
                || μ_prior(z | h_reduced) ) ]

Where:

h_full: expert observation history

h_reduced: reduced observation history

3.5 Freezing Priors

After training:

priors are frozen

never fine-tuned again

reused as regularizers

4. Stage 4 — Final Goalkeeper Policy

4.1 Purpose

The final policy learns:

when to move

which behavior to express

how strongly to commit

It does not learn raw motor skills.

4.2 Final Policy Interface

π_GK(z_t | o_t)

Observations:

proprioception

ball relative position

ball relative velocity

optional keeper pose relative to goal frame

4.3 Final Training Environment

Ball launcher environment:

ball launched from random position

random azimuth, elevation, force

Gaussian distribution centered on valid shots

shots may miss the goal

Episode ends when:

ball crosses goal line

ball is safely cleared

timeout

4.4 Reward Structure

Sparse Rewards

+1.0 → save (no goal)

-1.0 → goal conceded

Event Bonuses

+0.2 → deflection away from goal

+0.1 → clear beyond danger zone

Minimal Dense Shaping

penalty for ball velocity toward goal

small reward for contact when ball is on target

penalty for prolonged prone states

4.5 Using Priors During Training

Final policy is trained with KL regularization to a mixture of priors:

L_priors = KL( π_GK(z | h)
             || Σ β_i μ_i(z | h_i) )

J = J_RL - λ · L_priors

This allows:

soft switching between behaviors

smooth interpolation

no hard state machine

5. Training Order Summary

Stage 1: Train π_motor           → freeze
Stage 2: Train π_expert,i        → collect data
Stage 3: Distill μ_prior,i       → freeze
Stage 4: Train π_GK with priors  → final policy
